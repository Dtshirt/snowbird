Executive summary Deliverable 1

Google can reach every page, so this is not a blocked-crawler problem. Every one of the 92 URLs returns HTTP 200 with a correct self-canonical and index, follow. The problems are in what Google finds when it gets there, and in what customers find when they try to get a quote.

Two problems were costing you leads and trust right now. The quote form rejected anyone who typed a city like "Rochester, NY" and picked the matching suggestion, which is how most people fill it in; I reproduced this on the live site. And 7 of the 8 main route pages showed a made-up broker number, "MC #1234567", next to a placeholder phone number, (800) 555-1234. Both are fixed on the branch.

Biggest growth opportunities

  • Northbound routes. The brand is built for snowbirds going south and returning home, so this is core, not optional. Google Autocomplete shows people searching "ship car from Florida to New York", "…to New Jersey", "…to Michigan", "…to Massachusetts", "…to Ohio". You have no pages for any of them. The spring return (March–May 2027) is your first realistic organic season, so these pages need to be live by January.
  • Cost answers. The most common query shape is "how much does it cost to ship a car from X to Florida". Your cost guide is 438 words.
  • Amtrak Auto Train comparison. Snowbirds actively compare shipping against the Auto Train. You have no page on it.
  • A weak snowbird SERP. "Snowbird car shipping" results are broker blog posts and low-quality pages on pages.dev, helpscoutdocs and dev.to, with no dominant specialist. A credible dedicated site can compete there.

Why only ~12 pages are indexed and impressions are zero

Indexing and ranking are different problems, so I've separated them. I did not have Search Console access; the "about 12 indexed" and "33 crawl requests" figures are yours. Everything else below was measured.

Ruled out (checked and working)

  • Googlebot gets HTTP 200 on every URL. Server is plain nginx with no Cloudflare or bot challenge.
  • robots.txt allows everything except /api/ and points to the sitemap.
  • HTTP redirects to HTTPS with a 301; trailing slashes 308 to the clean URL; no www duplicate host.
  • Every page has a correct self-referencing canonical and index, follow.
  • Content, headings and links are server-rendered HTML with real <a href> links. JavaScript is not hiding anything.
  • Pages are fast enough: 33 KB of compressed HTML, layout shift 0.005, no heavy images.

Why Google is indexing so little

  1. Half the site was near-duplicate templates. 19 route pages, 22 city pages and 4 state pages share 82–92% of their text with each other. Google typically leaves pages like these as "Crawled – currently not indexed" and spends less crawl on a site that has many of them.
  2. Very little discovery signal. The site is two weeks old on a new subdomain, and your own parent site, carshippinghub.com, does not link to it at all. Low crawl demand matches the ~33 crawl requests you saw.

Why indexed pages get no impressions

  1. Competition. Route queries are dominated by established national brokers and data sites (MoveBuddha, FreightWaves, AmeriFreight, SGT, Ship A Car Inc). A two-week-old subdomain with no links rarely shows on page one or two for those yet.
  2. Vocabulary mismatch. The site leans on "seasonal auto transport" and "ship car to Florida for winter". Autocomplete returned no suggestions for those phrases. People type "ship car from New Jersey to Florida" and "how much does it cost to ship a car from NY to Florida".
  3. Missing half the demand. No northbound pages exist.
  4. Trust signals that a quality reviewer would flag. Invented reviews, a fake MC number and placeholder phone numbers are exactly what Google's quality guidelines tell raters to look for on a site handling people's cars and money.
  5. Timing. New pages often take weeks to earn impressions. No one can promise a timeframe, but the fixes above remove the reasons Google has to hold back.

Decisions only you can make 1, 3, 4 answered 31 Oct

I did not guess at any of these. Questions 1, 3 and 4 are answered and applied on the branch. Question 2 has one point left for counsel. The rest can follow the deploy.

  1. Phone confirmed: (888) 706-8784. It replaces (888) 555-7482 on every page, every Call button and the schema.Set once in data/site.js.
  2. The partner broker's name stays off the site, per your agreement. That can work as long as the name you advertise under is covered by the registration. For counsel: 49 CFR §371.7(a) bars a property broker from advertising "in any name other than that in which its registration is issued". The usual way to meet this while keeping the partner private is to have "Snowbird Car Shipping" (and "CarShippingHub") listed as trade names on the partner's FMCSA registration. Please confirm that is in place.§371.7(b) also requires advertising to show broker status; the site's broker note does that. No federal rule requires a property broker to show its MC number on a website (§371.107 covers household-goods brokers).
  3. Quote details go only to the carrier. The Privacy Policy now says so and states that you do not sell quote requests or pass them to other brokers, lead buyers or marketing companies.If your partner broker's staff see customer details while arranging a shipment, the policy should cover them too. It can do that without naming them.
  4. Deposit confirmed: 10% at booking, 100% refundable. Now stated in the homepage hero and benefits, the About page, the cost guide and the hidden-fees article."Balance at delivery" is kept as your site already said it.
  5. The consent checkbox is pre-checked and required. I recommend unchecked by default.A pre-checked box is weak evidence of consent for calls and texts under the TCPA, and auto transport lead forms are a frequent target of TCPA suits. One line in QuoteForm.jsx; your counsel's call.
  6. Do you ship from Canada?Autocomplete for "snowbird car shipping" leans Canadian (Toronto, Ontario). The homepage used to say you serve Canada; I removed that until you confirm, since cross-border moves involve customs.
  7. Where do the price ranges on route pages come from?They are presented as typical ranges. Checking them against your partner's real dispatch prices is the single most valuable accuracy improvement.
  8. Can carshippinghub.com link to this subdomain?It's the one authority source you control. A nav or footer link plus a contextual link from any snowbird or Florida content would help discovery.
  9. Terms & Conditions, cancellation, refund and damage-claim process.I removed the dead links rather than invent policies. Send the real terms and I'll publish them.
  10. Real reviews. Do you have any from the partner broker or past snowbird customers, and on which platform?Only genuine, attributable reviews can go back on the site (the FTC rule on fake reviews has been in force since October 2024).
  11. A named expert who can review content (an ops lead or dispatcher).A real reviewer name and bio on guides is the strongest E-E-A-T signal available to you.
  12. GA4 measurement ID and Search Console access for monitoring.

  • Keep the broker note next to every form; it answers "who am I dealing with?" at the moment of decision.

Tracking (implemented, needs your GA4 ID)

  1. Set NEXT_PUBLIC_GA_ID=G-XXXXXXX in the production .env and rebuild (it's read at build time).
  2. In GA4, mark generate_lead and phone_click as key events. quote_step_complete gives you the drop-off between steps.
  3. Qualified leads and bookings live in MongoDB (the status field on each quote). Track them weekly by pageUrl and routeSlug, which every quote already stores, so you can see which pages produce bookings rather than just form fills.
  4. Completed-call tracking needs a call-tracking number from your phone provider; a click is not a call.

In plain terms, what changed

We stopped Google from judging the site by 45 pages that said the same thing with different place names. We took down anything that wasn't true: the fake license number, the placeholder phone numbers, the made-up reviews and statistics. We added the pages a careful customer looks for before trusting a company with their car. And we fixed the form so people who type their city can get a quote. Next, we build the pages people are searching for that you don't have yet, starting with shipping the car home from Florida.
